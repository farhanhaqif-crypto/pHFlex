pH Classification (this system)

Strong acid: pH ≤ 3
Mild acid: 3 < pH < 7
Neutral: pH = 7
Mild base: 7 < pH < 11
Strong base: pH ≥ 11

University-level explanation

The pH scale is a logarithmic measure of hydrogen ion activity: pH = -log[H⁺]. A unit change corresponds to a 10× change in H⁺ activity. Strong acids dissociate substantially in water (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄), producing high [H⁺], while weak acids (e.g., acetic acid) only partially dissociate. Similarly for bases: strong bases (e.g., NaOH) fully dissociate, generating OH⁻.

In analytical chemistry, pH affects reaction equilibria (Le Chatelier), electrode potentials (Nernst equation), and speciation. When designing sensors, consider ionic strength and temperature since they alter effective activity coefficients and probe response.

Practical notes for measurements

- Calibrate probe with at least two standard buffers (pH 4 and pH 7 or pH 7 and pH 10).
- Rinse probe between samples; use fresh buffer solutions.
- Temperature compensation is important: electrode slopes change ~0.0035 V/pH/°C for ideal Nernst behavior.

Example calculations

For a solution with [H⁺] = 1.0×10⁻³ M, pH = -log(1×10⁻³) = 3.00 (strongly acidic). For a base producing [OH⁻] = 1×10⁻² M, pOH = 2.00 so pH = 14 - 2 = 12.00 (strong base).
